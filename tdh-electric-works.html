TDH Electric Works

Electric transportation and charging systems — the electrification arm of the TDH practice.

AdvertorialTDH Electric Works · a related entity of TDH International LLC

The question arrives at the airport manager’s desk in a form nobody trained for. A tenant wants chargers by the flight school. The city’s fleet plan says the ground-support tug is next. Somebody has heard there is money for this. And every one of those answers turns out to be a land use question before it is an electrical one.

Same desk, different discipline

TDH Electric Works exists because the practice kept meeting that question from the other direction. Its stated purpose is plain enough — “Dedicated to Powering the Future of Electric Mobility” — and the services it lists read like a planning scope rather than a contractor’s: consulting, project development, project management, grant writing, educational and community engagement.

That is not an accident of wording. Charging infrastructure fails on siting far more often than it fails on hardware. A pedestal in the wrong place is a pedestal inside a runway protection zone, or under an approach surface, or on a parcel whose lease does not permit it, or on a feeder that cannot carry the second phase. Those are the same constraints the rest of this practice spends its time mapping.

What it actually does

  • Consultation and advisory — infrastructure planning, charging station placement, and regulatory compliance.
  • Customised solutions — charging capacity, scalability, billing integration and fleet operations.
  • Green energy integration — pairing charging infrastructure with solar or wind generation.
  • EV charging infrastructure — installation across residential, commercial, fleet, parking and public sites.
  • Maintenance and support — proactive monitoring and dedicated technical support once stations are live.

The part most sponsors underestimate

Grant writing sits in the middle of that list rather than at the end of it, and that placement is the tell. Most electrification at a small airport is not funded out of the operating budget; it is funded out of a programme with an eligibility screen, a disadvantaged-community overlay, a match requirement and a deadline. Whether a given apron or parking lot qualifies is a mapping question. Whether the application survives review is a documentation question. Both are answerable before anyone specifies a charger.

TDH Electric Works states a focus on serving under-served and disadvantaged communities, and commits to working “with the highest levels of Integrity and Professionalism” toward “Transforming the World to Sustainable and Clean Energy projects.” For a small municipal field, the practical translation is that the screening overlays which decide funding eligibility are already familiar ground here.

Why it is on this site at all

Because the alternative is three vendors and three site plans that do not agree. An airport sponsor asking about fleet electrification, ground-support equipment or public charging in landside parking is asking a question that lands on the same desk as the Airport Layout Plan. When the siting analysis, the GIS base and the grant work come from one team, the charger location that gets funded is the same one that survives the Part 77 check.
